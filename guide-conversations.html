Conversation Starter Cards

Gentle prompts for
meaningful conversations.

For families navigating dementia, conversation can feel uncertain — you may not know what to say, or worry about saying the wrong thing. These prompts are designed to open doors gently, without pressure, and to meet your loved one exactly where they are.

01
Choose a question that feels natural for this moment — not every prompt fits every day.
02
Listen more than you talk. The goal is connection, not information-gathering.
03
If they don't engage, simply sit together. Presence is its own form of conversation.
Memories & the Past
Long-term memory often remains vivid longer than recent memory
01
"Tell me about a place you loved as a child. What do you remember about it?"
Childhood memories are often deeply preserved. This opens the door without pressure.
02
"What was your favorite meal growing up? Who made it?"
Food and scent are powerful memory triggers. This often brings warm, detailed stories.
03
"When you were young, what did you want to be when you grew up?"
Opens reflection on identity and dreams — a dignifying, affirming conversation.
04
"What was the music you loved most? Do you remember any of the words?"
Music engages a different part of the brain — even advanced dementia patients often recall songs.
05
"Tell me about how you met [spouse/partner]. What was your first impression?"
Deeply personal memories from young adulthood are often among the last to fade.
06
"What is something you were really proud of in your life?"
Affirms identity and dignity. Reminds both of you of who this person truly is.
The Present Moment
Simple observations about right now can spark genuine connection
07
"What's something that made you smile today, even something small?"
Grounds the conversation in today without requiring orientation to date or time.
08
"The light looks so beautiful right now. What does it remind you of?"
Sensory-based conversation requires no memory — just what they see and feel right now.
09
"Is there anything you'd like to do today, even something simple like a short walk or sitting outside?"
Gives them agency and choice — both of which matter deeply at every stage.
10
"You seem [peaceful / tired / thoughtful] today. How are you feeling in your body?"
Acknowledges their inner state without pressing. Opens physical and emotional check-ins gently.
Values, Wishes & Preferences
For conversations about care preferences and what matters most
11
"What makes you feel most comfortable and at home?"
Practical and affirming — helps care be shaped by their preferences, not assumptions.
12
"Is there anyone you've been thinking about lately that you'd like to reach out to or see?"
Honors their relationships and social connections, which remain meaningful throughout.
13
"What is something you'd like people to know about you that they might not know?"
A powerful dignity question. The answers often surprise and move caregivers deeply.
14
"If we could do anything together this week, what would you choose?"
Invites agency and imagination. The answer guides meaningful activity planning.
For Family Caregivers — Talking With Each Other
Conversations within the family that need to happen
15
"What part of this is hardest for you right now, honestly?"
Opens the door to honest family conversation rather than just logistics.
16
"How can I support you this week — not [loved one], but you?"
Names the caregiver's needs explicitly, which rarely happens on its own.
17
"Are there any decisions coming that we need to talk through together before they become urgent?"
Proactive planning conversation — easier before a crisis than during one.
18
"What do you think [loved one] would want if they could tell us right now?"
Recenters the family on the person receiving care — especially helpful during disagreements.
A Few Gentle Reminders
—Never correct or contradict. If they say something that isn't accurate, meet them where they are. The emotional truth of a moment matters more than the factual one.
—Silence is okay. A comfortable quiet between two people is its own form of intimacy.
—Repetition is not a failure. If they tell you the same story again, receive it as if it's the first time. The story still brings them joy.
—Your presence matters more than any question. Come as you are, without an agenda, and you have already given something meaningful.
