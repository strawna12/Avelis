Dementia-Focused Work

This work is
specialized.
We treat it that way.

The caregivers in the Avelis network are introduced specifically to families navigating dementia. That focus is intentional — and it shapes everything about how we operate.

If you have genuine dementia experience, you are compensated at the higher end of our range. Because that skill is rare. And rare things have value.

"The families we serve are in one of the hardest chapters of their lives. The presence you bring is not a small thing."
01
Meeting People Where They Are
Dementia care means entering someone's world — without correction, without urgency. You follow their lead. You honor their reality.
02
Calm as a Practice
A regulated caregiver regulates the person they're with. Your steadiness is not incidental to the care — it is the care.
03
Familiarity as Medicine
Routine, consistency, and a familiar face reduce anxiety for people with dementia more reliably than almost anything else. Your presence — showing up the same way, every time — is genuinely therapeutic.
04
Emotional Memory
A person may not remember your name. They will remember how you made them feel. Show up with warmth, every time — it lands, even when nothing else does.

What We Ask

Simple standards.
Genuinely held.

We are not heavy on rules. We are serious about a few things — because the families who trust us deserve that. Here is what being part of the Avelis network means in practice.

On independence: Caregivers in the Avelis network are independent contractors. You are not an employee. You set your schedule, accept the introductions you choose, and work with the autonomy that 1099 classification provides.
  • Show up when you commit. Families arrange their lives around these visits — reliability is the foundation of everything.
  • Bring genuine presence — not just physical attendance. The people in your care notice the difference.
  • Keep phone use limited during visits. Your full attention belongs to the person in your care.
  • Communicate promptly. If something shifts — your schedule, a client's condition — let us know quickly.
  • Maintain confidentiality. Everything you learn about a client and their family stays private — including social media.
  • Stay within non-medical scope. No clinical procedures. No medical advice. Observe and report — that is the line.
  • Approach each visit with the same warmth as the first. Familiarity should deepen care, not diminish it.
