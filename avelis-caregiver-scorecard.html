Temperament & Character
The most important section. Skills can be learned. Character cannot.

Emotional Composure Critical
Can they stay regulated under stress? How do they respond to confusion, repetition, or behavioral difficulty without showing alarm, frustration, or impatience? This is the single most important trait for dementia care.

Notes on Composure
Genuine Warmth Critical
Is their warmth authentic or performed? Do they show real curiosity about the people in their care — not just competence? People with dementia sense emotional authenticity even when they can't name it.
Weight ×2
Notes on Warmth
Patience Critical
Not just tolerance — active patience. Can they answer the same question warmly for the fourteenth time? Do they have the disposition to be unhurried even when things are hard?
Weight ×2
Emotional Maturity High
How do they handle feedback? Are they self-aware? Can they navigate complex family dynamics without taking things personally? Do they show good judgment about their own emotional limits?

Dementia Knowledge & Approach
Experience matters less than understanding. We can teach techniques — we cannot teach empathy.

Dementia Experience Critical
Direct experience with dementia or Alzheimer's clients — not just awareness. Have they worked with someone in the middle or late stages? Do they understand sundowning, wandering, behavioral shifts?

Notes on Dementia Experience
Person-Centered Philosophy High
Do they talk about clients as full people with histories and preferences — or as cases and conditions? Do they follow the person's lead, or impose structure? This reveals how they actually think about the work.
Weight ×2
De-escalation Instinct High
When a client becomes agitated or resistant — what do they do? Do they have a natural instinct to step back, give space, and redirect? Or do they push through?
